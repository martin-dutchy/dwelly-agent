## Procedure
1. Log the report as a repair job (category damp_mould) the same day. A report to the agency is notice to the landlord of the repair duty.
2. Ask for photos and where the damp is (which rooms, how large an area, how long), and whether anyone in the household has health problems, a child, a pregnancy or a breathing condition. One message.
3. Book an inspection by a suitable contractor or surveyor. Aim to inspect within about 10 working days, and sooner if there is a health risk. Ventilation and heating advice can go alongside the booking, never instead of it.
4. After the inspection, confirm the findings and the planned work to the tenant in writing, and send the landlord the findings with the work needed. Above the spend limit, ask the landlord's approval with the quote.
5. Get the landlord's decision on the repair. If the cause is a building defect (a leak, failed guttering, rising damp, a broken extractor) it is the landlord's duty to fix it.
6. Set a follow-up to check the work is done and the mould has not come back.
7. Legal position: Awaab's Law timescales apply to social housing only; they are not in force for private lets in October 2026. Do not quote them to a private tenant as a right. Treat damp and mould with the same urgency anyway: the landlord's repair duty and the fitness-for-habitation standard apply now.

## Required actions
- `create_ticket` or `update_ticket`: a damp and mould job with its severity.
- `request_information`: photos, rooms and household health.
- `assign_contractor` or `request_landlord_approval`: the inspection, within the spend limit or with approval.
- `send_message`: written findings and next steps to the tenant and the landlord.
- `schedule_follow_up`: inspection outcome and recurrence check.

## Red flags
- Children, a baby, someone pregnant, elderly, or with asthma or another respiratory or immune condition.
- Health symptoms the tenant links to the mould.
- Mould that came back after an earlier repair.
- The tenant mentions the council or environmental health.
- Water staining or a leak that may be the real cause.

## Escalate when
- A vulnerable occupant or any health symptom: P2, maintenance, after the job and inspection request are in place.
- A recurrence, a council or environmental health involvement, or a landlord refusing the repair: P2, maintenance, or P3, management for a complaint.
