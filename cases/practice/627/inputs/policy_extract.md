# Appliance repairs (agency)
- Appliances the landlord supplied (on the inventory) are the landlord's to repair. A broken oven or cooker is urgent: aim to restore cooking within 3 to 7 days, and keep the tenant updated while it waits.
- Warranty first: before any paid repair to an appliance fitted in the last few years, get the install date and the manufacturer's warranty terms from the installer. Manufacturer warranties often run 2 years or more; installer warranties are often 12 months.
- A quote above the landlord's limit, or any spend the landlord has asked to approve, needs the landlord's own approval before work is instructed or a visit for chargeable work is confirmed.
- Warranty repairs cost the landlord nothing; the manufacturer's engineer usually books directly with the tenant. Confirm the date with the tenant before telling anyone it is booked.
- Access: 24 hours' written notice for non-emergency visits. Agency keys are released to a contractor only with the tenant's written consent.
- Tenant recharges need evidence of tenant fault and the landlord's decision.
- Supplier bank details are never changed on the strength of a call, email or message: verify by calling the number already on file, hold the payment, and refer it to the fraud team.
- Product safety recalls and electrical hazards: tell the occupants to stop using the appliance and isolate it, tell the landlord the same day, and report it to the compliance team.

# AI phone line (contractor calls)
- Contractors are identified by the phone number on file (find_contractor).
- The phone line may take updates and record quotes and proposed dates on the ticket. It cannot approve spend or confirm a visit for chargeable work: what it recorded is for the case worker to check against the transcript.
