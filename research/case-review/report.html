Case review · 50 Northbridge Lettings maintenance cases · 1,505 events · 10 Oct 2026

Your maintenance jobs aren't slow because of the work. They're slow because nobody owns the next move.

We read every event in all 50 public maintenance cases and measured where the time, the human effort and the errors go. Then we ranked what to build by return on effort.

The answer
  1. 73% of a case's life is silence. Gaps of 3 days or more with no event add up to 1,029 of the 1,405 case-days. Most of that is unanswered contractors (524 days) and the agency itself not chasing (265 days). Landlords answer within hours when asked a clear question.
  2. 79% of human touches are mechanical. Of 353 human actions logged, 278 are relays, reminders, hold notes and status toggles an agent can do outright. 72 need a human decision that can be prepared for them. Only 3 truly needed a person from start to finish.
  3. The errors are the risk, not the speed. 11 of 50 cases sent the wrong property, address or document to someone. 28 contain a wrong or contradictory date. Money is approved without a price in at least 5 cases and invoices are never checked against quotes.

What to build: a case worker that always knows who owes the next move, chases on a clock, checks every message before it leaves, and hands a human a decision, never a chase.

Finding 1 · Time

The clock runs while everyone waits

Across 50 cases the median job needed one visit, yet stayed open 18 days. Landlords are rarely the bottleneck: case 049 got approval in 5 minutes, 050 in 30, 042 in an hour. The days disappear between messages, when a contractor has gone quiet or a hold note is re-filed without anyone being chased.

Finding 2 · Human effort

People are doing a router's job

We classified every human action in the 50 histories. The work is relaying a quote, forwarding an approval, sending a reminder, re-filing a hold, toggling a state the system got wrong. Dwelly already reports about 5 escalations and 15–20 minutes of human time per task. Most of what is left looks like this.

What we're building today

From review to a working case worker

Our hackathon entry already takes a case at any point in its history, decides the next action, performs it with tools, reads back that it happened, and escalates with a handover packet. Today we add the top three recommendations to it, so the review and the product tell one story.

Recommendation 1

Next-move owner

  • Every decision records who owes the next move and the deadline.
  • Chase, switch channel or re-dispatch instead of filing another hold.
Recommendation 2

Send guard

  • A check on every outbound message for property, person, date and amount.
  • A blocked send is logged and fixed, never silently sent.
Recommendation 3

Decision cards

  • Escalations become one question with options and drafted messages.
  • Paired with voice: a call becomes one more way to answer a card.
Method. Event timing, reply times, silent gaps and system prompts were computed by script from all 1,505 events in the 50 public Property Management cases. Human-touch classification, delay attribution, error categories and the case 047 counterfactual come from a full read of every case by five analysts working from the same template, and are estimates. The cases are synthetic exports provided by the hackathon organisers, so counts describe this sample, not Dwelly's live book. Dwelly volumes and minutes per task are from Dwelly's 3 Sep 2026 operations blog.
